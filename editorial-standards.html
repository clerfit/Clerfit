Editorial & Review Standards

How we document, test and communicate calculator methods.

1. Source selection

For health, nutrition and activity guidance, we prioritise primary research, systematic reviews, government public-health agencies, national academies, recognised standards bodies and professional organisations. We link to original research or official guidance where practical. Sources are selected for relevance to the method being described.

A source may support a formula family without validating every implementation detail or every population to which a calculator might be applied.

2. Formula transparency

Where applicable, calculator pages should identify the equation or method, define inputs and units, and explain important assumptions. Estimated outputs should be described as estimates. Site-defined ratios and mathematical descriptions should be labelled accordingly rather than given invented clinical meaning.

3. Testing and quality checks

Calculation code can be checked with known-answer examples, unit-conversion comparisons, input-boundary tests and browser interaction tests. Accessibility and responsive-layout checks are also part of the engineering process. Passing an automated test demonstrates only what that test covers; it does not prove clinical validity, complete scientific accuracy or suitability for every individual.

4. Professional review and credentials

ClerFit does not imply that a page has been medically or professionally reviewed unless a qualified reviewer has completed that review. Where professional review is obtained, the page should state the reviewer's name, relevant credential, review date and exact scope. A developer, automated test or cited paper must not be described as a clinical reviewer.

5. Limitations, age and population applicability

Equations can vary in accuracy across age groups, sexes, populations, measurement protocols and health circumstances. Adult reference categories must not be applied to children or teenagers when age- and sex-specific standards are required. Where an appropriate reference standard is unavailable, ClerFit should present the calculation without inventing a universal normal range or risk category.

6. Updates and corrections

When a source, equation, implementation or limitation changes, the relevant explanation and reference should be reviewed. Users can report suspected errors through the Contact page. Please provide the calculator name and a non-sensitive example; do not send medical records or private health details.

7. Independence and endorsements

Listing a government agency, research journal, university or professional organisation as a source does not mean that it endorses, certifies, sponsors or has reviewed ClerFit. Any relationship or endorsement will be stated only when real and verifiable.
