What is Relative Fat Mass (RFM)?

Relative Fat Mass (RFM) is a body composition metric used to estimate body fat percentage using a person’s height and waist circumference. It focuses on abdominal fat, which is strongly linked to health risks.

Applications of RFM

  • Estimates body fat percentage without special equipment
  • Identifies abdominal and central obesity
  • A complementary estimate; it does not replace validated body-composition assessment
  • Tracks fat loss and fitness progress
  • Used in health screening and medical studies
  • Helps in diet and exercise planning
  • May help track estimates over time when measurements are taken consistently
  • Applicable for both males and females
  • Quick and simple calculation
  • Supports long-term health monitoring

Limitations

RFM estimates body-fat percentage from waist, height and a sex-based equation. It is not a direct measurement and should not be used by itself to diagnose body-fat excess, cardiovascular risk or metabolic disease. Measurement technique and population differences affect accuracy.

Calculation methodCommon RFM equations: male estimate = 64 − 20 × (height ÷ waist); female estimate = 76 − 20 × (height ÷ waist), using compatible length units.
