BMR / Resting Energy Estimate

What this estimate represents

This calculator uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, originally developed to predict resting energy expenditure (REE), not a direct measurement of basal metabolic rate (BMR). Consumer tools sometimes use this equation as a BMR estimate, but basal and resting metabolic rate are measured under different conditions and should not be treated as identical.

Potential uses

  • May serve as one starting input to a separate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) estimate when an activity assumption is stated.
  • Can help explain the difference between resting energy and energy used across a normal day.
  • Is not a total daily calorie target or a personalized nutrition prescription.

Limits and health cautions

  • The original Mifflin–St Jeor study measured resting energy expenditure in healthy adults aged 19–78; the result is a prediction with meaningful error.
  • Prediction uncertainty may be greater for people outside the study population or with conditions not represented in it.
  • This calculator cannot diagnose thyroid disease, fatigue, or a metabolic disorder from a calculated value.
  • When a precise energy requirement is clinically important, seek guidance from a qualified clinician or registered dietitian.
Understand the resting-energy estimate +

What does this calculator estimate?

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation predicts resting energy expenditure (REE). It is not a direct measurement of BMR and does not represent the energy used across a full day.

What does the result mean?

It is an approximate resting-energy prediction. A separate activity assumption is needed to estimate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), and neither number is a personalized calorie prescription.

Calculation methodMale REE = 10 × mass(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5. Female REE = 10 × mass(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161.

The original Mifflin–St Jeor study measured REE in healthy adults aged 19–78. Formula predictions have error and should not be treated as metabolic measurements.

Potential use

Use as one starting input in a separate TDEE estimate, with the activity-factor assumption made explicit.

Limitations

Accuracy varies by person and population. This calculation cannot diagnose thyroid disease, fatigue, or a metabolic disorder.

How to use it

Read each field label and its unit, enter your measurements or values, check that none are missing or implausible, and activate the calculator button. Review the displayed result, formula and limitations. If comparing results over time, use the same measurement method.

Important: ClerFit provides educational calculations. It does not replace professional medical, nutrition or exercise advice. Check the calculator's formula and units before relying on a result.
