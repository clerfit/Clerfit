Calculator Guide +

How to measure

Vitamin B12 intake is estimated from your food and supplement record. Check nutrition labels and serving sizes for foods such as animal-derived foods and B12-fortified products. Include supplements and enter the typical daily total in micrograms (mcg). This is an intake estimate, not a blood B12 test.

How to calculate

  1. Choose your age and reference sex.
  2. Select pregnancy or lactation if applicable; these options are available for female profiles aged 14–50.
  3. Add the B12 amounts from the servings you consumed and enter the daily total in mcg.
  4. Calculate to compare the total with the selected RDA.
FormulaRDA percentage = daily B12 intake (mcg) ÷ selected B12 RDA (mcg) × 100

The RDA is selected using age and life stage. The reference sex field is retained for consistency with the shared intake calculator.

Understanding the result

The percentage is a comparison with a population reference intake. It cannot tell whether your body absorbs B12 normally or whether your blood level is low or high.

Limitations

Food-label estimates may not capture actual absorption or all sources. No tolerable upper intake level (UL) has been established for B12, so the calculator does not display a UL comparison. These U.S. reference values are for healthy populations aged 14 and above, not a substitute for clinical assessment.

Method and limitations

Percentage of RDA = entered daily intake ÷ selected age-, sex- and life-stage-specific RDA × 100.

These U.S. Dietary Reference Intake values are intended for healthy populations aged 14 and above. An RDA comparison is not a diagnosis of deficiency, adequacy or toxicity. Individual needs may differ with medical conditions, absorption, medications and clinical circumstances.

Vitamin A total retinol activity equivalents (RAE) are not compared with the UL because the UL applies only to preformed retinol. No UL has been established for vitamin B12. Vitamin B6 and C UL values, where shown, are context only and are not intake targets.

Reference sources: U.S. National Institutes of Health, Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) health professional fact sheets for vitamins A, B12, B6 and C.
