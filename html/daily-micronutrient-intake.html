Calculator Guide +

How to measure

Use a food diary or a reliable nutrition tracker for a typical day. Check package labels and serving sizes, and include food, fortified foods and supplements. Enter each vitamin in its displayed unit: vitamin A as mcg RAE, B12 as mcg, and B6 and C as mg or mcg using their individual selectors. Do not count a full package if you ate only one serving.

How to calculate

  1. Choose your age and the reference sex used by the published table.
  2. If relevant, select pregnancy or lactation; those options are available only for female profiles aged 14–50.
  3. Enter the daily total for each vitamin, converting micrograms to milligrams for B6 and C when needed.
  4. Calculate. ClerFit compares each vitamin separately with its matching reference amount.
FormulaPercentage of RDA = daily intake ÷ selected RDA × 100

For B6 and C, mcg is converted to mg by dividing by 1,000. The RDA is selected separately for each vitamin using age, reference sex and eligible life stage. No combined nutrition score is calculated.

Understanding the result

The percentage shows how the entered daily amount compares with a U.S. Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for a healthy population. It is a reference comparison, not a personal diagnosis or a measure of the quality of your whole diet.

Limitations

Values depend on accurate food records, serving sizes and label data. These U.S. reference values are for ages 14 and above and may not fit every country, medical condition or individual need. Vitamin A total RAE is not compared with a UL; no UL is established for B12. B6 and C ULs are context only, not targets. Ask a qualified health professional about individual nutrition or supplement decisions.

Method and limitations

Each nutrient is compared separately with its own age-, sex- and life-stage-specific Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA). Percentages are not combined into an overall nutrition score.

These U.S. Dietary Reference Intake values are intended for healthy populations aged 14 and above. An RDA comparison is not a diagnosis of deficiency, adequacy or toxicity. Individual needs may differ with medical conditions, absorption, medications and clinical circumstances.

Vitamin A total retinol activity equivalents (RAE) are not compared with the UL because the UL applies only to preformed retinol. No UL has been established for vitamin B12. Vitamin B6 and C UL values, where shown, are context only and are not intake targets.

Reference sources: U.S. National Institutes of Health, Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) health professional fact sheets for vitamins A, B12, B6 and C.
