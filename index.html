BMI (Body Mass Index)

Founder: Adolphe Quetelet

Definition: BMI measures body fat based on your weight in relation to your height. It helps categorize underweight, normal, overweight, and obesity levels to monitor health risk.

Formula:

Weight (kg)
--------------------
Height (m)2

Category

CategoryBMI Range
Underweight< 18.5
Normal18.5 – 24.9
Overweight25 – 29.9

  • Underweight
    • Weak immunity
    • Anemia
    • Osteoporosis
    • Nutrient deficiency
  • Normal
    • Low risk
    • Generally healthy
  • Overweight
    • Hypertension
    • Type 2 diabetes
    • Joint pain
    • Stress
  • Obese
    • Heart disease

    • High blood pressure
    • Metabolic syndrome
  • Severely Obese
    • Very high risk of cardiac arrest
    • Diabetes
    • Sleep apnea
    • Mobility issues

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)

Founder: Harris & Benedict

Definition: BMR estimates the number of calories your body burns while at complete rest to maintain vital functions such as breathing, circulation, and cell production. It helps in planning diets, managing weight, and understanding energy needs.

Male:
BMR = 88.36 + (13.4 × Weight kg) + (4.8 × Height cm) - (5.7 × Age years)

Female:
BMR = 447.6 + (9.2 × Weight kg) + (3.1 × Height cm) - (4.3 × Age years)

Category

Activity LevelMultiplier
Sedentary1.2
Lightly active1.375
Moderately active1.55
Very active1.725
Extra active1.9

Types of Health Problems Due to Low or High BMR

  • Low BMR:
    • Weight gain despite low food intake
    • Fatigue
    • Slower metabolism
    • Difficulty losing weight
  • High BMR:
    • Unintended weight loss
    • Rapid heartbeat
    • Insomnia
    • Nutrient deficiencies

Body Fat Percentage

Founder: William H. Durnin & J. Womersley

Definition: Body Fat Percentage (BFP) indicates the proportion of your body weight that is made up of fat. It helps evaluate fitness, monitor health risks, and design personalized diet and exercise plans.

For Men: BFP = (1.20 × BMI) + (0.23 × Age) - 16.2
For Women: BFP = (1.20 × BMI) + (0.23 × Age) - 5.4

  • High Body Fat:
    • Cardiovascular diseases

    • Metabolic syndrome

Ideal Weight

Founder: Devine & Robinson

Definition: Ideal Weight estimates the weight you should maintain for optimal health based on height, gender, and body composition. It guides weight management, fitness planning, and reduces disease risk.

Male: Ideal Weight (kg) = 50 + 2.3 × (Height in inches - 60)
Female: Ideal Weight (kg) = 45.5 + 2.3 × (Height in inches - 60)

Calorie Needs

Founder: FAO/WHO/UNU (Dietary guidelines)

Definition: Calorie Needs calculate the total energy your body requires daily based on BMR, activity level, age, and gender. It helps plan diet, control weight, and optimize performance.

Total Calories = BMR × Activity Factor
Activity Factor: Sedentary=1.2, Lightly Active=1.375, Moderately Active=1.55, Very Active=1.725, Extra Active=1.9

Protein Intake

Founder: Recommended by WHO / FAO

Definition: Protein Intake calculates the daily protein requirement based on body weight, activity level, and fitness goals. It helps build muscles, repair tissues, and maintain overall health.

Protein (g/day) = Body Weight (kg) × Protein Factor
Protein Factor: Sedentary=0.8, Active=1.2–1.7, Bodybuilding=1.6–2.2

Waist-Hip Ratio (WHR)

Founder: World Health Organization

Definition: WHR measures fat distribution by comparing waist and hip circumferences. It identifies risk for cardiovascular and metabolic diseases more accurately than BMI alone.

WHR = Waist Circumference (cm) ÷ Hip Circumference (cm)

FFMI (Fat-Free Mass Index)

Founder: Kouri et al.

Definition: FFMI measures muscularity adjusted for height. It estimates the amount of lean mass relative to body size and is useful for athletes and bodybuilders to track muscle development.

FFMI = (Lean Mass (kg) ÷ Height (m)²)

Relative Fat Mass (RFM)

Founder: Woolcott & Bergman

Definition: RFM is an alternative to BMI for estimating body fat using height and waist circumference. It is more accurate for identifying fat-related health risks across genders.

Male: RFM = 64 – (20 × Height ÷ Waist)
Female: RFM = 76 – (20 × Height ÷ Waist)

Category

CategoryRFM Range (%)

Types of Health Problems

  • High RFM:
    • Obesity
    • Diabetes
    • Heart disease
    • Joint pain
  • Low RFM:
    • Low energy reserves
    • Weak immunity
